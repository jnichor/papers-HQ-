**Rationale for referee selection:**

- **CREDIBILITY**: The paper's central empirical claim hinges on a dramatic fade-out (Cohen's *d* = 0.88 → near-zero). A credibility referee should probe attrition patterns more carefully (5–8% overall, but the endline salary Lee bounds span zero), whether the 18-month follow-up is long enough to distinguish true fade-out from delayed effects, and the uneven arm sizes (control *n* ≈ 400 vs. training arms *n* ≈ 280), which deviate from the optimal allocation for a factorial design.

- **POLICY**: The paper draws strong prescriptive conclusions ("policymakers can deploy interventions independently," "bundling yields no synergy") from a single-country, all-male sample. A policy referee should evaluate external validity to other MENA contexts, the welfare interpretation of the LFP persistence finding, and whether the cost-effectiveness framing is adequately developed given that wage vouchers are expensive per job-month created.
