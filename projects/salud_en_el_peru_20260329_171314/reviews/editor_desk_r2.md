**Guidance for referees:**

**CREDIBILITY referee** should focus on: (1) the cross-sectional design and whether the authors' descriptive framing adequately distances the paper from causal claims — several passages in the introduction and conclusion slide toward causal language; (2) the insurance assignment mechanism — SIS is means-tested, EsSalud is employment-based, so selection into insurance is highly endogenous with the outcome; (3) whether the placebo test (random reassignment) is informative at all given the authors' own admission it is "weak"; (4) the "regressive protection" interpretation in the conclusion, which requires ruling out that higher-quintile SIS enrollees are targeting-leakage cases with systematically different health risk profiles.

**MEASUREMENT referee** should focus on: (1) the EsSalud N=648 issue — the authors attribute this to a variable construction error but do not resolve it; this invalidates a third of the insurance typology; (2) the mechanical circularity: consumption quintiles are constructed from total consumption, which includes health spending, creating a built-in positive correlation between quintile and OOP share that the authors acknowledge but do not correct; (3) the ×13 annualization factor and whether the sensitivity to ×12 is sufficient; (4) whether the P41603 "other health costs" heterogeneity warrants disaggregation; (5) the two-part model referenced in the introduction but apparently not implemented as a primary specification.
