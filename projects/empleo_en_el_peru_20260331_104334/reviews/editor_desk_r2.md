## Desk Review Decision

**PROCEED TO REFEREES**

### Rationale

The paper clears the bar for external review on all five dimensions:

- **Scope/fit**: Sharp, well-defined research question with direct policy relevance. Peru's 70%+ informality rate and the 4-year post-pandemic window make this a high-value empirical setting. The "scarring vs. recovery" framing is clean and tractable.

- **Methodological soundness**: The Dingel-Neiman teleworkability DiD is a credible identification strategy, now well-established in the COVID-19 labor literature. The authors are unusually candid about the design's limits (no pre-2020 data, contaminated 2020 baseline, rotating panel structure limiting true within-individual tracking). This candor is a strength, not a weakness.

- **Data adequacy**: ENAHO is the appropriate data source. N=262,733 is more than adequate. The 96.9% attrition by 2024 is severe but the non-differential attrition result ($p=0.163$) partially addresses the concern.

- **Presentation**: Well-organized, clearly written, proper referencing, limitations disclosed.

- **Contribution**: A 4-year panel-based causal estimate of informality scarring in a major developing economy is a genuine contribution. The social-security vs. firm-size channel decomposition adds policy bite.

The main vulnerabilities — no pre-trend test, crosswalk validity, the near-zero coefficient on the contract-based informality measure — are legitimate refereeing issues, not grounds for desk rejection.

**Referee selection rationale:**

- **CREDIBILITY**: The paper's load-bearing weakness is identification — no pre-trend validation is possible, the 2020 baseline is partially treated, and the "scarring" interpretation leans on population-level rather than within-individual evidence. A credibility referee will pressure-test the DiD assumptions and assess whether the authors' transparency about these limits is sufficient.

- **MEASUREMENT**: The divergence across informality definitions (social security: 0.089; written contract: ~0.002; small firm: 0.047) is the paper's most substantive puzzle and is currently underexplained. The crosswalk from U.S. SOC to ISCO-08 for Peru also needs scrutiny. A measurement referee will assess whether the outcome variable is doing what the authors claim and whether the channel interpretation holds.
