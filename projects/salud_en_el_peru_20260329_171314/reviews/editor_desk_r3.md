**Desk Review Assessment**

**Scope and fit**: Strong. The paper addresses a well-defined and policy-relevant question—whether insurance financial protection in Peru varies across the OOP spending distribution—using nationally representative data. The "regressive financial protection" framing is novel relative to standard binary-threshold CHE analyses.

**Methodological soundness**: Competent and self-aware. The quantile regression framework is appropriate, survey weighting is handled correctly, and the restriction of interpretation to τ ≥ 0.50 given the 44.1% zero mass reflects genuine methodological literacy. The two-part model complement is appropriate. Importantly, the authors themselves flag two issues as requiring resolution: (1) the EsSalud variable is almost certainly miscoded due to household-head-only assignment (~2% coverage vs. expected ~30%), contaminating the uninsured reference category; and (2) consumption quintiles are constructed from total consumption inclusive of health spending, mechanically inflating the quintile–OOP gradient. These are remediable data construction problems, not fundamental design flaws.

**Data adequacy**: ENAHO is the appropriate dataset. N = 33,691 is adequate. The 2024 wave introduces post-pandemic adjustment caveats, acknowledged by the authors.

**Presentation quality**: Above average. The paper is well-organized, technically careful, and unusually transparent about its own limitations. This transparency is an asset, not a liability.

**Potential contribution**: Meaningful if the measurement issues are resolved. Distributional analysis of insurance effects in Peru's fragmented system fills a genuine gap.

**Decision**: PROCEED. The paper meets minimum standards—it has a coherent question, appropriate methods, adequate data, and transparent execution. The two flagged issues are data reconstruction problems, not identification failures. Referees are better positioned than the desk to judge whether the corrected quintile construction changes the gradient meaningfully and whether the SIS-only restriction is sufficient.

Referee selection: the dominant risks are (1) whether the identified associations survive measurement correction and (2) whether cross-sectional conditional quantile associations can bear the causal weight implied by the policy narrative. These call for a MEASUREMENT referee and a CREDIBILITY referee.
